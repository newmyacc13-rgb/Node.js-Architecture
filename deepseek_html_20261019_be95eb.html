Engineering note · 2026.11 Ingest / Admission / Tail latency

Backpressure without the tail.

Static queue depths are tuned for the median and paid for at p99. Saturate's admission controller tracks service-time variance instead — holding the line without holding the queue.

01 — The queue is not the problem

Most ingest systems treat backpressure as a queue-depth problem. The queue is a symptom. The signal that matters is the second derivative of service time: how quickly the distribution is widening, not how many requests are parked. A depth-256 queue at steady state and a depth-256 queue three milliseconds into a variance spike look identical to a gauge. They are not identical to the tail.

Saturate's controller samples a rolling service-time window and estimates the 95th percentile of the next window from the current dispersion. When dispersion crosses a threshold, admission narrows before the queue fills. The result is a system that sheds early, cheaply, and without the correlated timeout cascade that follows a full queue.

02 — Admission tracks variance

The controller replaces a fixed max-inflight cap with a variance-scaled envelope. At low dispersion the envelope opens; at high dispersion it closes faster than a token bucket because it is responding to the shape of the distribution rather than a count of permits. The config surface stays small:

admission.toml 
# variance-scaled admission — 2026.11 defaults
[admission]
  mode            = "variance"
  window_ms       = 8
  max_inflight    = 256
  shed_threshold  = 0.94
  recovery_ms     = 140

[queue]
  depth           = 1024
  drain_batch     = 32
  fairness        = "deficit"

[shed]
  strategy        = "earliest-deadline"
  retry_after_ms  = 25

03 — What changes at the edge

At the edge the controller runs in a smaller envelope. The window shortens, the recovery is more aggressive, and the shed strategy biases toward deadline preservation rather than throughput. The table below is the operating envelope we ship for edge ingest:

04 — Field notes

Two failure modes show up repeatedly in production. Neither is solved by a bigger queue.

Cold-start variance

A freshly scaled node has no service-time history. The controller seeds from the cluster median and widens its uncertainty band for the first 140 ms. Without that seed the node over-admits on its first burst and immediately sheds.

Tenant fairness

Deficit round-robin keeps one noisy tenant from consuming the whole envelope. The controller accounts for per-tenant debt at drain time, not at admission time, so a bursty tenant cannot starve a steady one.
